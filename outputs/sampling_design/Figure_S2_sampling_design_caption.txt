Figure S2. Sampling hierarchy for Arabis alpina on Mount Kenya. (a) The 200-m-wide belt followed an established route from 3,000 to 4,800 m, extending 100 m perpendicular to each side. Eighteen contiguous 100-m elevation bands were surveyed. Two 100 × 100 m blocks per band and ten plots per block gave 36 surveyed blocks and 360 surveyed plots, including unoccupied units. Arabis alpina occurred in nine blocks and 27 plots (3,763–4,567 m); 27 blocks and 333 plots were unoccupied. Dashed horizontal lines mark elevation-band boundaries and triangles identify occupied plots. (b) Two illustrative pairs show one block on each side of the route within each band. Blocks are 100 × 100 m; the full belt is 200 m wide. The illustrated ground buffer separates successive block rows, not the paired blocks across the route, and represents the minimum 20-m ground distance rather than a 20-m elevational interval. Within each block, ten non-overlapping 5 × 5 m plots were randomly positioned using coordinate pairs along perpendicular axes from a fixed reference corner O, with at least 10 m between plot boundaries (Bureau of Land Management, 1996). All ten plots are shown in each of the four illustrative blocks. Generated coordinates and block identities are schematic. (c) Well-spaced rosettes representing different plant sizes were selected in occupied plots. Each separately rooted rosette was a sampling unit; stolon-connected rosettes were not assumed to be genetically distinct. The dataset contains 581 sampled rosettes across 27 occupied plots. The detail does not imply a fixed number of plants per plot. The mountain and allocation of ground blocks to elevation bands are conceptual; they are not a map or a physical slope profile.
