## 2.7.5 Germination responses to source elevation

To assess H7a-H7d, we fitted separate Gaussian mixed-effects models for time to first germination (T0), germination time spread, mean germination rate (MGR) and germination percentage (GP), with standardised source elevation as the fixed-effect predictor and random intercepts for block and source plot. Positive elevation coefficients for T0 and negative coefficients for MGR and GP were consistent with H7a, H7b and H7c, respectively. Time spread was assessed without a directional prediction (H7d).

Because GP is derived from germinated counts out of 100 seeds, timing was recorded in daily intervals and time spread was bounded by the 34-day observation period, we did not assume that Gaussian errors were appropriate solely because the responses were numerical. We examined conditional residual Q-Q plots, residuals against fitted values and scale-location plots. We then assessed sensitivity using binomial and beta-binomial mixed models for germinated and ungerminated counts, cumulative-logit ordinal mixed models for T0 and time spread, and a Gamma mixed model with a log link for MGR. All comparisons used the same 581 accessions, full-precision responses, elevation scaling and block/source-plot random-intercept structure. Fixed-effect confidence intervals and two-sided P values were obtained using Wald approximations. We inspected numerical convergence, Hessian definiteness, finite standard errors and boundary variance estimates. Ordinal fits also required a maximum absolute gradient below 0.0001; fits failing this criterion were not treated as confirmatory sensitivity evidence. For the binomial, beta-binomial and Gamma models, uniformity, dispersion and outlier checks used 1,000 DHARMa simulations, with P < 0.05 identifying a diagnostic departure. Gaussian coefficients were retained as original-scale benchmarks, with interpretation qualified by these checks.

## Results: model diagnostics and sensitivity

Gaussian residuals showed marked skewness for T0 and time spread, so normal-error assumptions were not supported uniformly across the four responses. The negative elevation association with GP persisted in both the binomial model (log-odds coefficient per elevation SD = -0.722, 95% CI -0.863 to -0.580) and the beta-binomial model (-0.721, 95% CI -0.862 to -0.580). The Gamma model likewise retained a negative elevation association with MGR (log-mean coefficient = -0.155, 95% CI -0.188 to -0.122). All three effects had P < 0.001, and their DHARMa uniformity, dispersion and outlier tests had P > 0.05. The Gamma model had a boundary random-effect variance estimate.

The ordinal timing models gave positive elevation coefficients, agreeing in direction with the Gaussian estimates. However, neither met the specified gradient criterion, and the time-spread model also produced a warning during random-effect updating. These fits therefore do not establish robustness of inference for T0 or time spread. The Gaussian timing associations should be interpreted as provisional rather than as results supported by satisfactory diagnostics across alternative model families.

## Discussion: qualification of germination findings

The negative associations of elevation with germination percentage and mean germination rate were insensitive to the alternative response families examined. Evidence for later first germination and wider germination time spread was less secure because Gaussian residual departures were accompanied by numerical limitations in the ordinal sensitivity fits. These timing associations should therefore be interpreted cautiously.
