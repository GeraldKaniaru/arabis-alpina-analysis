FIGURE 4

Elevational associations and trait relationships

Read manuscript caption

Figure 4. Elevational associations and trait relationships in A. alpina. Panels show (a) elevation and flowering onset, (b) elevation and flowering duration, (c) onset and final flowering date, (d) onset coefficients for final flowering date within and among plots, (e) elevation and MISM, (f) elevation and total seed number, (g) MISM and total seed number, and (h) mass-number coefficients within and among plots. MISM denotes mean individual seed mass (mg per seed). In a-c and e-g, points represent plants, lines show model predictions and shading indicates 95% confidence intervals. Solid and dashed lines denote P < 0.05 and P ≥ 0.05. In d and h, points show coefficients and bars indicate 95% confidence intervals. Reference lines mark zero and one in d and zero in h. For final-date coefficients in c and d, zero indicates no association with final date and one indicates unchanged duration with later onset. The P value in c tests a slope of zero. Coefficients are expressed per 100-m elevation increase (a, b, e, f), one-day delay in onset (c, d) or 1-mg increase in MISM (g, h). Panel e uses natural-log-transformed MISM, and f-h coefficients use the log-link scale. Flowering day 1 is 1 June 2021. The fitted line in c represents fixed effects with random intercepts set to zero. R2m and R2c denote marginal and conditional R2. Symbols ns and *** denote P ≥ 0.05 and P < 0.001.

Elevation, seed mass and germination

Read manuscript caption

Figure 5. Associations of source elevation and seed mass with germination responses in A. alpina. Panels show coefficients from (a) elevation-only models and (b) 100-seed-mass-only and elevation-adjusted mass models. Circles, squares and diamonds identify these model sets, respectively. FIGURE 6

Baseline and projected reproductive traits

Read manuscript caption

Figure 6. Baseline and projected reproductive traits of A. alpina for June 2021 and 2041-2060. Panels show (a) flowering onset, (b) flowering duration, (c) mean individual seed mass (MISM) and (d) total seed number per plant. Grey open points show baseline means. Filled brown and yellow points show equally weighted ensemble means under SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, respectively, and open future points show individual climate-model means. Error bars span the 2.5th-97.5th percentiles of plant-level predictions. They describe variation among predictions, not uncertainty in mean changes. Lines connect baseline and future means. Annotations report changes in response units and, except for onset, as percentages of baseline means. Onset changes are expressed only in days because percentages depend on the reference date. Table 3 and Fig. S6 report bootstrap confidence intervals for mean changes. Seed-number predictions use the revised nbinom2 model without random intercepts and retain all environmental predictors.

FIGURE S2

Sampling design

Read manuscript caption

Figure S2. Sampling design for flowering observations and seed collection in A. alpina. (a) The transect followed an established route from 3,000 to 4,800 m, with sampling on both sides. The survey covered 18 elevation bands, with two blocks per band and ten plots per block, giving 36 blocks and 360 plots. Triangles identify the 27 occupied plots across nine occupied blocks spanning 3,763-4,567 m. (b) Within each block, ten non-overlapping 5 × 5 m plots were randomly positioned using coordinate pairs measured along perpendicular axes from a fixed reference corner, with at least 10 m between plot boundaries. Adjacent blocks were separated by unsampled buffers of at least 20 m measured along the ground between block boundaries. (c) Within occupied plots, well-spaced rosettes representing different plant sizes were selected. Each separately rooted rosette was treated as a sampling unit, without assuming that rosettes connected by stolons were genetically distinct. In total, 581 rosettes were sampled. Arabis alpina occurred in 27 plots across nine blocks. Positions and distances are schematic and not drawn to scale.

FIGURE S5

Spearman rank correlations

Read manuscript caption

Figure S5. Spearman rank correlations among environmental variables and reproductive traits. Tile values show Spearman’s correlation coefficient (ρ), with green and brown indicating positive and negative correlations, respectively. Soil PC1 summarises standardised soil properties. Onset and duration refer to flowering. T0, time spread (Tg – T0), MGR and GP denote germination onset, spread, mean rate and percentage. Only the lower triangle is shown. Blank cells identify untested pairs, namely germination responses with MISM or total seed number, and 100-seed mass with all variables other than the four germination components. MISM equals 100-seed mass divided by 100, so their rank correlation is 1 by construction. MISM is mean individual seed mass in mg per seed. Asterisks identify Benjamini-Hochberg-adjusted P values: *, P < 0.05; **, P < 0.01; ***, P < 0.001. Unmarked values have adjusted P ≥ 0.05.

FIGURE S6

Projected changes in reproductive traits

Read manuscript caption

Figure S6. Projected changes in A alpina reproductive traits for 2041-2060 relative to June 2021. Panels show (a) flowering onset, (b) duration, (c) MISM and (d) total seed number per plant. Brown and yellow identify SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5, respectively. Violin widths show the estimated density of mean changes from 1,000 parametric bootstrap replicates.
